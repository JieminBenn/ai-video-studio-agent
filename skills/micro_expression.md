# Micro-Expression Performance Skill

Purpose: make emotional video shots feel acted instead of posed.

Use temporal facial beats:

- Start state: the face before the emotion breaks through.
- Trigger: the tiny visual cause of change, such as a glance, breath catch, swallowed word, or remembered detail.
- Eye behavior: focus shift, blink timing, eyelid tension, wetness, pupil/highlight direction, and gaze avoidance.
- Brow and forehead: compression, asymmetry, lift, pinch, or release.
- Mouth and jaw: lip pressure, tremble, parted breath, swallowed line, teeth tension, or forced smile.
- Breath and voice: inhale/exhale timing, throat tension, pause length, and whether the line lands cleanly or cracks.
- Body echo: shoulders, neck, hands, spine, weight shift, and how emotion leaks into posture.
- Resolution: what the expression becomes at the end of the shot, ready for the next cut.

Write these as short time-coded beats when the shot duration allows. Keep the acting specific but filmable.
Avoid melodramatic overacting, frozen expressions, random tears without cause, and face-only emotion with no breath or body response.
